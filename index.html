[ 04 ]The question everyone asks

"So why don't we make chocolate?"

Because the best chocolate on earth already exists. It's made in the valley, by the families who grow the fruit. We lived with one of them. They have the medals to prove it.

And here's what they taught us: they eat the whole fruit. The pulp. The strand. The husk. This idea wasn't born in a lab. It was born at their table.

But even perfect chocolate has a limit: it uses the seed. 20% of the plant. The other 80% has no market. Every family in cacao knows it. Nobody had built the answer.

Chocolate, at its best, is 20% of the story.

We are the other 80. Not against chocolate. Beyond it.
We don't compete with their chocolate. We complete their fruit.

[ 05 ]The anatomy of the fruit

One fruit. Four parts. One kept.

THE HUSK
THE PULP
THE OAT
THE SEED
01The husk~70%Discarded

The thick ridged shell. Most of the fruit by weight. Dumped in piles at the field edge, where the discard feeds next year's disease.

02The pulp~9%Discarded

The sweet white flesh around every seed. The part the families eat first. It never leaves the jungle.

03The oat~1%Discarded

The strand that ties the seeds through the core. Milky, oat-like, almost impossible to save without field systems. Nobody has ever sold it.

04The seed~20%The only part they keep

Fermented, dried, roasted, shipped. The single part of the plant the entire chocolate industry is built on.

They keep the 20%. We take the rest. — that is the whole roadmap: 18% today → 30% → 99% → 100% → the tree → the plant
