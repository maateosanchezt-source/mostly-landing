[ 02 ]The question everyone asks

"So why don't we make chocolate?"

Because the best chocolate on earth already exists. It's made in the valley, by the families who grow the fruit. We lived with one of them. They have the medals to prove it.

And here's what they taught us: they eat the whole fruit. The pulp. The strand. The husk. This idea wasn't born in a lab. It was born at their table.

The industry is another story. It buys harvests rotten, roasts them beyond taste to hide the damage, and calls a sugar bar with barely 10% cacao "chocolate". That version shouldn't exist.

But even perfect chocolate has a limit: it uses the seed. 20% of the plant. The other 80% has no market. Every family in cacao knows it. Nobody had built the answer.

Chocolate, at its best, is 20% of the story.

We are the other 80. Not against chocolate. Beyond it.
We don't compete with their chocolate. We complete their fruit.
That's why we're The Non-Chocolate Cacao Brand.

[ 03 ]The anatomy of the fruit

One fruit.
Four parts.
One kept.

01The husk~70%The thick ridged shell. Most of the fruit by weight. Dumped at the field edge, where the discard breeds next year's disease.
02The pulp~9%The sweet white flesh around every seed. The part the families eat first. It never leaves the jungle.
03The strand~1%The cord that ties the seeds through the core. Milky, oat-like. Nobody has ever sold it.
04The seed~20%Fermented, dried, roasted, shipped. The single part of the plant the entire chocolate industry is built on. — the only part they keep

They keep the 20%. We take the rest.

[ 06 ]The outsider

Built by an outsider.

I'm Mateo.

School gave up on me early. I couldn't sit still, couldn't follow the slow room, and the report cards said what they said. So I went looking for the one place where a fast brain is an advantage. I found the kitchen.

I started cleaning fish at five in the morning. Years later I was cooking in some of the best kitchens on earth. And one night, plating something perfect for twelve people who could afford it, I understood I was making beautiful things that changed nothing.

So I left. Sold what I had. Went to the Peruvian jungle to learn about a fruit.

A family there took me in. The Tupayachis. They grow cacao, they make some of the finest chocolate in the world, and they eat the whole fruit — the pulp, the strand, the husk. Everything the industry throws away, they had been eating at their table for generations. That is where this started. Not in a lab. At their table.

I'm not building a supplement company. I'm building the thing I needed, from the part of the fruit nobody wanted, with the people who taught me what it was.

Everything I have is in this. There is no plan B.

On 10.10, I'll show you the first one. Take a number. Walk in first.

— Mateo
